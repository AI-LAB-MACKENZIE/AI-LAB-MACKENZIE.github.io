Drills

An app for people who want to really learn a language: to understand it and speak it on their own, in the shortest possible time.

← Back to Work

The project

There are hundreds of language apps, and most treat learning as a game: points, daily streaks, loose exercises. They are good for keeping the habit, but rarely get anyone to converse, read or work in the new language. Drills starts from a different question: what is the shortest path from knowing nothing to getting by on your own?

The idea comes from Fellipe Silva Martins and Carlos Eduardo Pereira. The app is used by ear, in sessions of a few minutes, and all the course content is planned and organized with the help of artificial intelligence.

01

Serious learning

A complete course, planned from the first day to the last, designed to bring people to real use of the language rather than to entertain.

02

Bootcamp format

Intensive and to the point: giving learners as much independence as possible in the shortest possible time.

03

Understanding and speaking

The focus is on comprehending and producing the language, not on the traditional study of grammar rules.

Why start with Japanese

The first course is in Japanese on purpose, an extreme case for Portuguese speakers: a different script, a different word order, almost no words in common. If the method works there, it is expected to work even better in closer languages. The most sought-after languages come next.

The project has the support of Mackenzie's China Studies Center (NECH), which is helping develop the Mandarin Chinese version.

Where it stands

Drills is at the stage before the minimum viable product (MVP). There is no public version yet, and the details of how it works will wait until the app is ready to be shown. In the Lab, the project connects to the innovation research area and to the tool development line of action: it is an idea born in research that aims to become a product.

Next steps

  1. Development

    Building the app and the content of the first course.

  2. MVP

    A first complete, lean version with the essentials to be used for real.

  3. Testing

    Use by a group of people, to measure what works and fix what does not.

  4. Incubation

    Application to a business incubator, possibly one of the Mackenzie incubators.

  5. Funding

    Seeking resources to grow. One possibility is the FAPESP PIPE program, which supports innovative research in small businesses.

Innovation stage

Drills is currently at stage 2: the prototype works and the problem is being validated. The goal is to reach stage 3, with the MVP ready and the first real users, which is the starting point for the incubator and the funding application.
