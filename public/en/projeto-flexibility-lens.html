The project

What makes someone flexible when a project has to change course? Each professional answers that question with their own criteria. For some, a flexible person is one who sees many alternatives; for others, it is one who notices that the situation has changed and acts quickly. Flexibility Lens helps each person discover their own criterion and shows what that view tends to overlook.

The app builds on the results of the third paper in Lidiane da Silva Souza's doctoral thesis, supervised by Fellipe Silva Martins in the Graduate Program in Business Administration. The study surveyed 210 professionals who work in project teams and used the PAPRIKA method to estimate the weight they give to four dimensions of cognitive flexibility: awareness of alternatives (what), processing agility (how), sensitivity to context (where and when) and willingness to act (who). On average the dimensions weigh almost the same, but hardly anyone thinks like the average: participants fall into seven profiles, each prioritizing a pair of dimensions.

In the app, the person goes through the same choices as in the study. On each screen, they compare two hypothetical people who differ in only two dimensions and say which one thinks more flexibly. In about three minutes, they get the weight they give to each dimension, their closest profile, the opposite profile and separate suggestions for managers and for team members. In the team view, members pool their result codes and see which profiles and dimensions the team covers.

Makes visible that people on the same team judge flexibility by different criteria, and offers a shared language to talk about it.

Decision-making

Puts a multi-criteria decision analysis into practice: the weights come from pairwise choices, as in the PAPRIKA method.

 03

Teaching and research

Turns the result of a thesis into a tool that managers, students and teams can use to reflect and talk.

How it works

  1. Pairwise choices

    Between 7 and 13 comparisons of two hypothetical people, using the same level descriptions as the study. A repeated question checks that the answers are consistent.

  2. Optional self-assessment

    Four items on how the person usually acts. The app compares what they value with how they see themselves and points out development priorities.

  3. Diagnostic

    The weight of each dimension compared with the average of the 210 participants, the closest profile, what that view tends to overlook and the profile that complements it.

  4. Team view

    Each member shares a short code with their weights. Together, the codes show the profiles present, the coverage of the four dimensions and the axes of opposition in the team.

Using it with care

Flexibility Lens is a tool for reflection. The diagnostic describes what a person considers important in someone flexible, not how flexible they are. The results are not a psychological or professional assessment, have no legal value and must not be used in hiring, promotion, performance review or any other decision about people. The authors and developers are not responsible for the results or for how they are used. The seven profiles come from an exploratory cluster analysis and should be read as tendencies.

The app runs in the browser and does not send answers to any server. The code is open, in the lab's GitHub repository. The app is available in Portuguese and English.

To cite the app: Martins, F. S., & Souza, L. S. (2026). Flexibility Lens: a diagnostic of views of cognitive flexibility in project teams [Software]. https://github.com/AI-LAB-MACKENZIE/flexibility-lens

Innovation stage

Flexibility Lens is currently in phase 2: the app works as a prototype, with the study's real data. The target is phase 3, with use by project teams and in the classroom.
