GroupCognito

A simulation platform for studying how groups of people reach (or fail to reach) a decision.

← Back to Work

The project

Committees, boards, project teams: many decisions in organizations are made in groups. Who persuades whom? How much does it matter to have better information than one's peers? Does the network of relationships help the group get it right, or only make it agree faster? GroupCognito is a virtual laboratory for these questions.

On the platform, each group member is an agent with its own personality (based on the Big Five model), charisma, experience and information-access profile. The agents are connected in a social network, exchange opinions over several rounds and change their minds under the influence of others. Ties grow stronger when agents agree and weaker when they disagree. The researcher sets the group size, the type of network, the share of well-informed agents and the pace at which new information appears, and follows the outcome round by round.

Everything can be adjusted. The network can be complete, random, Erdős-Rényi, Watts-Strogatz, Barabási-Albert or custom-built to mimic a real or fictional organization. Each agent can be configured individually: personality, charisma, experience, need for closure, information seeking, privileged access to information and an information profile covering quality, volume, diversity, timeliness and technical knowledge.

Beyond single simulations, the platform runs batch experiments, with thousands of runs and parameters varied in a controlled way, and delivers the statistical analysis of the results. It also includes an AI experiment designer: the researcher describes what they want to investigate, and the AI helps define the simulations and runs them. Everything can be done through the interface, with no programming, but those who prefer can also use the platform through code.

The starting idea is simple: a group's decision emerges from the meeting of each person's cognition, their personality traits, the information each one holds and the social structure that connects them. These factors are usually studied separately, and there is a lack of models showing how they interact over time. For this reason the project brings together three fields: Information Science, Social Network Analysis and Behavioral Strategy. The platform grew out of a partnership between the Graduate Program in Business Administration and the Graduate Program in Information Science at the University of São Paulo (PPGCI-USP).

The platform was presented as a technical-technological product (PTT) at EnANPAD 2026, with the paper Plataforma de Simulação Baseada em Agentes para Apoio à Tomada de Decisão em Grupo (Agent-Based Simulation Platform for Analyzing Group Decision-Making Strategies), published in the ANPAD proceedings. This page shows that stable version. A new version is already in development.

How a round works

Before the first round, the platform builds the network and creates the agents, with default, random or individually set values. Then, in each round, it repeats the same cycle:

  1. Informational evidence

    Each agent forms a judgment from its own information, measured on six dimensions (quality, volume, diversity, timeliness, technical knowledge and experience). They are combined by geometric mean, so a very weak dimension pulls the whole down and cannot be offset by the others.

  2. Social influence

    The agent looks at its neighbors in the network. Each one's opinion is weighted by the speaker's charisma and the strength of the tie between the two.

  3. Opinion update

    The new opinion combines the evidence, the social influence and anchoring in the previous opinion. Each agent's personality sets how much each of these parts weighs.

  4. Recording and adaptation

    Each change of opinion is recorded as an influence event (who persuaded whom), and the network ties grow stronger or weaker according to agreement.

  5. Group growth

    If the researcher wishes, new agents join the group each round.

At the end, the platform calculates consensus, decision accuracy and network metrics, applies PageRank and HITS to the influence history and shows everything in more than 15 interactive charts, ready to export.

What the platform does

  1. The social network

    Complete, random, Erdős-Rényi, Watts-Strogatz, Barabási-Albert or custom networks that mimic an organization, with ties that adjust as agents agree or disagree.

  2. Custom agents

    Each agent can have its own personality, attributes and information profile, or the group can be generated with default or random values.

  3. How opinions evolve

    Charts and tables with each agent's opinion per round, the formation of consensus and the flow of agents between opinions.

  4. The weight of information

    A comparison between well-informed agents and the others, to see whether those who know more can lead the group to the right decision.

  5. Who influenced whom

    A directed graph of actual influence, showing the agents who persuaded others the most and those who were listened to the most.

  6. Batch experiments

    Thousands of simulations at once, with independent and dependent variables defined by the researcher. The platform handles the statistics: descriptives, correlation, regression, ANOVA and t-test, with heat maps, scatter plots and distributions, and helps interpret the results.

  7. AI experiment designer

    An assistant, connected to language models from OpenAI or Anthropic, that turns a research question into an experiment setup and runs it.

  8. Data for analysis

    Export of results and settings in JSON and CSV, to repeat the experiment or take the data to other tools.

Impact

The platform is already used in research and teaching. So far, it has led to:

  • one doctoral thesis;
  • two papers submitted to journals;
  • use in the classroom, to show in practice how groups decide;
  • a code repository, closed for now. Because the platform is tied to a doctoral thesis, the code will be opened after further testing, so that other researchers can check and reproduce the simulations.

Innovation stage

GroupCognito is currently in phase 2: the stable version works, has already been used in research and has been reviewed at a scientific meeting. The goal is to reach phase 3 with the new version, ready to be used by other researchers and professors outside the lab.
